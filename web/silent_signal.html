Silent Signal

We computed forty acoustic indices for 759,767 bird recordings spanning a decade, then asked whether any of them predicts how many species are actually present. None of them does. This is the record of that null result — and of what the data measures instead.

Result 01 — validation

No acoustic index tracks species richness

Each index is averaged per grid cell and correlated against S_rare10 — species richness rarefied to ten recordings, so that a cell with 900 recordings is not simply credited with more species than one with 12.

Across 11,088 adequately-sampled cells, every correlation sits inside ±0.09. The strongest, the noise-removed Bioacoustic Index, explains under 1% of the variance. For comparison, an index that genuinely captured site biodiversity would be expected well above 0.3.

Spearman ρ — cell-mean index vs. effort-controlled richness

n = 11,088 cells · axis fixed to ±0.30

Why the axis runs to ±0.30. Drawn to the data's own range these bars would look substantial. Held to the range a useful predictor would need, the result reads correctly: they are all close to zero.

Result 02 — correction

Two of those correlations were artefacts

22,007 recordings decoded to silence. Indices that divide by zero returned blanks, but the two still mathematically defined on an all-zero signal returned a hard 0 — and those zeros were being averaged into cell means.

Effect of excluding degenerate rows

ρ before → after

NB peaks — previously reported as the strongest predictor at ρ = +0.132 — fell to +0.077. The Acoustic Diversity Index collapsed from +0.066 to −0.003: that correlation was entirely artefactual.

The remaining nine indices moved by no more than 0.0002, and richness itself was untouched — cell counts and rarefied richness are bit-identical before and after.

The guard keys on ZCR = 0, which real audio never produces. It excludes exactly one row wrongly, while preserving 11,311 genuine zero-peak recordings and 203 genuine zero-diversity ones. Dropping all zeros would have replaced one artefact with its opposite.

Result 03 — where the data is

Coverage is recordist coverage, not ecological coverage

Every recording with coordinates carries an ecoregion, biome and realm, assigned by point-in-polygon against RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017 and cross-checked against the WWF 2001 layer. The two agree on biome for 96.7% of them.

12,382 half-degree cells, coloured by dominant biome

Europe is a single saturated band. Temperate Broadleaf & Mixed Forests alone holds 3,023 of the 12,382 cells plotted here — 14,065 on the underlying 0.1° grid — and 42% of all recordings; the three smallest biomes together hold 365. Every per-biome number below inherits this imbalance — a comparison between biomes is partly a comparison between recordist communities.

Result 04 — change over time

No biome changes direction over the decade

Splitting the yearly richness series by biome produces deltas ranging from −3.8 to +1.9. None of them survives contact with its own year-to-year variance.

Median richness by biome and year

blank = fewer than 5 scored cells · hover for values

Each biome on its own

Read the grey bars first. Where recording effort is small the line is jagged; where it is large the line is flat. That is the entire pattern.

The tracked cells settle it. Of individual cells followed across two or more years, 1,111 rose and 1,099 fell. That symmetry, in every biome, is the fingerprint of observer turnover — not of directional ecological change.

Result 05 — the human axis

City and rural richness are indistinguishable

Recordings were classified city, town or rural against the Global City & Town Boundaries polygons. The acoustic indices separate these classes cleanly. Richness does not separate them at all.

Median richness by settlement class

shaded years: class carried forward, no polygons after 2022

Over the window where the classification is real (2015–2022) the city median moves −0.2 and rural +0.2 — both far smaller than either series' jitter. Tracked cells split city 109/103 and rural an exact 945/945.

What the indices did show. Rural recordings run markedly higher on ACI (2301 vs 1500) and NDSI (0.62 vs 0.40) than city ones. So the indices respond to the urban gradient while failing to track richness — consistent with them measuring recording conditions rather than biodiversity.

Caveat — data quality

Completeness is uneven, and it is uneven by geography

94.4% of labelled recordings carry all forty index columns. But the missing 6% is not scattered at random — it tracks where the European processing runs failed.

Share of recordings complete on all 40 metrics

Mediterranean Forests drops to 84.8% and the Palearctic realm to 91.8%, against 96–98% everywhere else. Any biome comparison inherits an uneven sample, and the worst-affected biome is the third largest.
